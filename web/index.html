The Sync Box is sending, but its data cannot be read

Data is arriving on the Sync Box port, but none of it can be decoded, so the lights are not following the picture. This usually means the box's firmware changed what it sends.

Press Download diagnostics and attach the file to an issue on the project's GitHub page, together with your box's firmware versions (from its app). The file holds counters and a few raw frames (colours and sizes only: no addresses). If you kept a capture from before the update, tools/check_capture.py can compare it with the new one (see docs/RESILIENCE.md).

1. Connect to your Wi-Fi

The board restarts and joins your network. Then open http://fancy2wled.local (or the address your router shows) from a device on the same network. It must be on the same network/VLAN as the Sync Box.

Targets

A target is a run of LEDs on a WLED that follows one part of the picture. Add one for each strip, for example a strip behind the TV and a cabinet light. The cards below edit the target you pick here; the Look settings are shared, and each target has its own brightness.

Picture map

Which part of the picture each target follows. The grid is the Sync Box's 6 by 4 zones. A coloured block means that target's LEDs follow that zone; the darker the block, the more of its LEDs follow it (the corners usually come out darkest, because the stretch of strip past the TV holds the corner colour). The faded middle zones are never used: the box only drives the edges. Hover or tap a block for the numbers. The map follows your changes before you save them.

2. The whole run: how long is it?

The run is the stretch of your strip you want the TV to control; the rest of the strip is left alone. For example, if the first 210 LEDs of the strip are behind and beside the TV: first LED 0, length 210.

3. Which part of the run covers the TV?

Give the strip's own LED numbers where the TV starts and ends (). Along that part the colours follow the picture exactly; the LEDs before and after it fade smoothly (see Smooth beyond the TV below). Leave both empty if the whole run is exactly as long as the TV.

When you change these numbers, red LEDs light up on the strip where the TV starts and ends, and blue ones where the run starts and ends.

Look

The Sync Box smooths its own colours (about 0.14 s per step), which is most of the delay between the picture and the lights. Reaction boost undoes that smoothing: 0 follows the box exactly, 100 reacts straight away. At high values fast flashes look sharper and a dark scene can flick to black a little early.

LEDs along the TV follow the picture exactly. LEDs past the edge of the TV show the colour of the corner zone at their end of the strip (for LEDs on the ceiling: the top-left zone at the left end, the top-right zone at the right end), reaching it at the end of the strip. 100% = it takes the whole strip, lower = it blends faster, 0 = the end LEDs just repeat the TV's edge colour.

When the TV is off

The Sync Box sends no signal of its own when the TV is switched off (checked with the TV's power button, over CEC): it keeps sending, with a completely black picture. So the board decides the TV is off when the picture has been completely black for a while, and on again with the first colour. A long black screen counts as off too, which is why the delay is a setting.

Home Assistant (MQTT)

The board connects to your MQTT broker (for example Home Assistant's Mosquitto add-on) and appears in Home Assistant by itself as a Fancy2WLED device: a Sync light (on/off and brightness), status sensors and the Look settings. The board connects out to the broker, so your firewall must let it reach the broker (TCP, usually port 1883).

Off by default. When on, the board measures its CPU load, memory and frame timing and publishes them every 5 seconds to <topic prefix>/perf, plus a set of diagnostic sensors in Home Assistant. The measuring itself uses a little CPU, so switch it off when you are done: it then stops measuring and publishing, and the sensors disappear from Home Assistant. Needs MQTT to be connected.

Back up / move to another board

Export saves your WLED address, LED layout, phone calibration, Look settings and MQTT settings to a file. Import loads such a file onto this board, for example a new ESP32 after you have set up its Wi-Fi. The Wi-Fi name and password are never included (a new board joins your Wi-Fi first, then you import).
